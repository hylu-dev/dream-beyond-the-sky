# Cliff impact framing and sound review

The storm's old white wash through the cloud wall is gone. From the storm's start to the blow, an approach effect builds with how far she has flown toward the impact point (`approach` in `src/crash.js`): the fall's red-black vignette closes in (clear centre 80% to 34%, up to 0.88 opacity), colour drains by up to 60%, contrast rises by 45%, a slight blur creeps in, the field of view narrows by up to 12 degrees and the camera trembles. Over the last 20% of the way it surges: the view narrows a further 6 degrees and the tremble triples. There is deliberately no heartbeat here; the fall's own heartbeat starts after the blow. The ramp follows distance raised to the 1.2 power, so it is felt early and keeps rising until the hit; the impact flash and the falling dread take over from it.

At contact the camera turns 55 degrees toward the open right side with a modest 24-degree upward tilt, retaining a strip of cliff face beside the sky, with a small directional roll. She leaves the frame. The sideways angle holds for 0.28 seconds, then blends down into the running fall camera by 1.35 seconds. A small vertical toss (3 m/s upward, 9.8 m/s² gravity) gives the camera a rise and drop; its offset blends out from 0.55–1.35 seconds. The camera no longer tries to catch her at the frame edge during impact. The established close-up lens and subsequent falling framing remain.

Her outward recoil starts at 7 m/s (previously 11), with horizontal drag increased from 1.4 to 1.8 per second. This reduces her first-second horizontal travel by at least 30%, keeping the fall closer to the cliff. The underlying fall camera follows this shorter trajectory. During recovery (0.65–2 seconds), a temporary distance guard keeps the lens outside her tumbling body and brings its aim onto her current position; the old wall-held aim lag could otherwise lose her below frame at about 1.2 seconds. After 2 seconds the original falling camera is unchanged. The menu’s crash-preview shortcut uses the normal flight render layer.

A strong immediate white flash, 7 px blur, contrast/desaturation and brightness pulse, and tighter darkened edges land with the sound and sky cut. The daze holds for 0.06 seconds and clears by 0.65 seconds in real time, independently of the brief hit stop, before the existing falling dread effect builds.

The current hit is panned toward the cliff (-0.45 for the left-side impact), with the falling rush slightly opposite (+0.2). The pre-impact gasp is removed. The hit briefly ducks other sound to 12% for 0.18 seconds, then restores it over 0.9 seconds. The selected **03 — Deep canyon collision** is now installed, with +2 dB gain and a gentle +1.5 dB low shelf at 120 Hz. Its pitch and natural decay are preserved; no synthesized bass sweep is added. The prepared master is retained by the audio processing manifest.

`/art/audio/impact-review/` offers four new ElevenLabs performances, an existing-layer remix and the previous recording. The selected audition includes the approved volume and bass lift; other auditions retain their comparison levels. Each can be played alone or with consistent approach/fall sounds. Favorites do not change the game.

Validation: `node tools/verify-impact-camera.mjs` exercises the real crash/flight/camera logic and cliff geometry at 30, 60 and 120 Hz across three headings. It checks unchanged FOV, the upward snap and screen pulse on the contact frame, the camera rising and dropping while she leaves the frame, reduced recoil against the previous physics, sound direction, absence of the gasp, cleanup, and safe framing during recovery and equality to the underlying fall camera after 2 seconds. Rendered browser review covers the contact, 0.18, 0.35, 0.65, 1.2 and 2.2 seconds after it. These checks do not replace listening to choose an impact timbre.

After a 2.45-second silent black hold, a full-frame black overlay fades away from 0.15–4.1 seconds after waking, with no blinking or opening slit. The cinematic letterbox bars remain. Initial blur is 36 px and clears over 6.5 seconds, continuing into the wide shot. Her gasp and panting use a separate recovery bus: 28% volume, a 650 Hz low-pass and soft distortion at first, easing to full level, 20 kHz and no distortion by 6.5 seconds. Ambient sound stays independent; later breaths are clear. The bus restarts on each wake and pauses with the audio context.

The added exhale at 11.2 seconds was removed from playback and preloading after review. The cues follow the `sigh` clip's measured pelvis: her backward step stops against the trunk at 2.08 seconds into the clip (13.28 s), and she drops 0.46 m down it from 2.75 to 3.3 seconds (13.95–14.5 s). The cloth/body-to-bark contact is cut to its first thump (the take knocked again 0.45 s later, mid-slide) and starts 0.25 s early so the thump lands on the stop. The slide uses two newer takes (3 and 6), each a single clean rub of about half a second that starts with the drop and fades as she sits; the earlier takes were so quiet at source that bringing them to level made them hiss. The last settling breath follows 0.1 s into the slide. Once she sits, the clip takes one more breath through her lips (shoulders rising 3.6–4.1 s, falling 4.2–4.6 s as the mouth relaxes). Only its breath out is heard: the quiet nose exhale (`wake-settle`, at 40%) at 4.25 s. An inhale at 3.6 s was tried and dropped (three breaths in 1.4 s was too much), as was a mouth exhale. From the cut in for the deep breath (9.2 s) on, her voice is placed 3 m further off than the camera (`WAKE_UP.end.voiceBack`), so it is quieter, darker and a little more open-air in the wider shots instead of sounding close-miked.

`breathe.glb` (the clip she wakes in) had a motion-capture skate: between 4.53 and 4.87 s her whole body, feet and all, slid 0.93 m forward with the feet still on the ground, read as a jerk at the end of the blurred close-up, and the crossfade into `sigh` at 11.2 s then slid her about 0.9 m back again. The pelvis translation track was patched in place to cancel the feet's average forward drift over that span and hold the offset afterwards, so her feet stay planted while she straightens and draws them together; the crossfade into `sigh` now moves her feet only 3 cm. The deep-breath shot's camera is unchanged (moving it 0.9 m in to keep its distance on her was tried and rejected), so she now stands about 0.9 m further from it than before. Faster arm movements remain near 5.3–5.5 s, after the cut to the wide shot.
